Nuclear Oil & Gas / Mining Transit Insurance

Nuclear

$2M of screening and tagging work saved per year

Condition report screening and functional failure tagging

Situation
A nuclear station logs thousands of condition reports and work orders a year. Each one has to be screened, and any functional failure has to be identified and tagged for equipment reliability reporting.
Constraint
Data stays on the site network. The screening committee keeps the decision.
What I built
I tuned a model on the station's past screening and tagging decisions. It proposes the functional failure tag, code and priority, and shows the similar past records behind each suggestion. A custom evaluation harness I built around it scores each release against committee decisions and feeds corrections into a self-improvement loop.
Controls
Low-confidence items go to full manual review. Every suggestion is logged, and the model is re-evaluated before any update ships.

Operator safety risk model

Insurance

Self-improving research agent for competitor and regulatory filings

Situation
Analysts researched competitor and regulatory filings by hand, and quality varied from one analyst to the next.
Constraint
Every finding must trace to its source, because it feeds pricing decisions a regulator may question.

How I work with clients

Most engagements cover two stages: deciding which AI use case is worth funding, then building it to production under your controls. Where a client has already selected the use case, the work starts at the second stage.

Stage 1 · Strategy

Select the use cases worth funding

  • I rank candidate use cases on two criteria: business value and the effort required to gain approval. A valuable use case that cannot pass security review has limited near-term value.
  • I assess what your data, infrastructure and AI-use policy permit today.
  • I produce a roadmap that both executive sponsors and engineering leads can endorse.
  • Where vendors are under consideration, I evaluate their proposals against your controls before you commit.

Stage 2 · Implementation

Build systems that pass review

  • A custom harness around the model, covering retrieval, tool access, guardrails, human review and audit logging.
  • Evaluation sets developed with your subject matter experts, against which every release is scored before deployment.
  • Fine-tuned models for specialized tasks where general-purpose models fall short.
  • A continuous improvement loop that turns reviewer corrections into new test cases.

A staged engagement with a decision at each step

  1. 1

    Assess

    I map the workflow, the available data and your existing controls, and we select the one or two use cases worth proving.

    Decision Fund a proof of value, or conclude with a written roadmap.

  2. 2

    Prove value on your data

    Your experts and I define the evaluation set before any system is built, so the acceptance criteria are independent of the solution. I then demonstrate performance against that set on your own records, within your environment.

    Decision Your reviewers confirm whether it meets the acceptance criteria.

  3. 3

    Build and transition

    I harden the harness, integrate review and audit logging, and document the change control process before handing the system to the team that will own it.

    Sign-off Formal approval for production use.

Guiding principle: your own experts define the evaluation set before anything is built. It becomes the standard your reviewers apply at sign-off.

Questions your security and compliance teams will ask

I design the answers into the system from the start rather than addressing them after a pilot. In my experience, AI pilots in regulated environments are more often delayed by security and compliance review than by model performance.

  • Does our data leave our environment? Only if you approve it. Systems are deployed on your network or in your cloud tenancy; on the nuclear screening engagement, all data remained on the site network.
  • Which models are used? Only models on your approved list. No model is substituted without your approval.
  • Who approves the output? A designated role signs off before any output informs a decision. On the insurance research engagement, an actuary approves all findings before they reach pricing.
  • Is there an audit trail? Yes. Inputs, outputs, sources and reviewer actions are all logged.
  • How are new versions validated? Every version is scored against your evaluation set before release.
  • How are model and prompt changes controlled? They follow the same change control and review process as any other system change.
  • Can outputs be traced to their sources? Yes. Every answer links back to the source record or document.
